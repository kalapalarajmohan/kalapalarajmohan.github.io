where this stands right now

In test deployment, my own room — not yet back with the person it's for

The unit is currently running in my own room, not my mother's. Two honest reasons it hasn't gone back yet: first, the packet capture is bursty — 1 to nearly 3,000 packets/second, averaging ~30/s — which distorts frequency-based extraction like breathing rate if treated as evenly-sampled; I'm currently integrating a Lomb-Scargle periodogram (built for exactly this kind of irregular timing) to fix that properly before trusting numbers from it again. Second, and bigger: gross movement is already resolvable, but fine-grained posture and pre-pain guarding are not yet accurate enough to justify calling this equivalent to what a camera would show — that gap is the open research problem, not a bug I expect to close alone.

the constraint

No wearables. No cameras. That's not a design choice — it's a condition.

My mother has chronic pain and refuses to wear a monitoring device or be recorded on camera. I wanted to know if she was in pain at night without asking her to accept either. That's the entire brief this work has been answering since April 2026.

system

Contactless CSI-based health monitoring

WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) — the way a room's walls, furniture, and bodies distort a WiFi signal — carries enough information to extract breathing rate, gross movement, and (in principle) posture, with no device on the subject.

FINDING — VERIFIED

checked against ground truthBreathing-rate extraction was independently validated on the actual deployment hardware, not just simulated — 26 of 30 sampled windows agreed within 5 bpm of a reference measurement.

FINDING — NEGATIVE RESULT, KEPT

didn't hold upA kernel-SVM posture classifier reached 86% accuracy in simulation. On real data, posture classes in the deployment room produced statistically indistinguishable subcarrier ratios — the classifier doesn't work here. This stayed in the public repo instead of being quietly dropped.

The unsolved problem: gross repositioning (centimeter-scale) is resolvable with this setup. Pre-pain micro-muscular guarding — the signal that would actually matter — is micrometer-scale, sitting in the same 1–30 Hz band with no fixed reference frame during sleep. That gap is what the next phase of this work is about.

second project

IMU-triggered fall-detection airbag

Closing the 15–100m fall-height dead band
2026 — hardware built, algorithm tested, deployment mechanism identified

Most fall-detection systems are tuned for very short or very long falls. Falls in the 15–100m range — a person on a ladder, scaffolding, or a low roof — are underserved. This project detects freefall duration and gyroscopic tumble, then triggers a fast-deploying airbag.

0–15m short-fall detectors
15–100m the dead band — this project
100m+ parachute-class systems
  • Detection algorithm built and tested in Python; ready to port to an Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense (LSM9DS1 IMU).
  • Miura-ori origami folding identified as the deployment mechanism — reliable, fast, and mechanically simple to fold flat when not in use.
